# Training — The Week

**Lift Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Upper body. 45–60 minutes a session.**

Three sessions, matched to the lift days the meal plans already feed. Push day, pull day, then a mixed day so every muscle gets hit twice a week — the frequency the hypertrophy research points to. Log every set in **Hevy** (free tier is enough): three routines set up once, then the job each session is simply to beat the log.

| Day | Session | Focus | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** *(lift)* | Push A | Chest, shoulders, triceps | ~55 min |
| **Tue** | Rest or easy cardio | 20–30 min rower/bike, conversational pace | Optional |
| **Wed** *(lift)* | Pull B | Back, rear delts, biceps | ~55 min |
| **Thu** | Rest or easy cardio | 20–30 min rower/bike, conversational pace | Optional |
| **Fri** *(lift)* | Upper C | Everything, lighter and higher rep | ~50 min |
| **Sat** | Off | — | — |
| **Sun** | Off | — | — |

**The rules that matter, in order:**

1. **Progressive overload.** When you hit the top of the rep range on every set of an exercise, add weight next time (smallest jump available — 1–2kg on dumbbells, one pin on the cable stack). That's the whole engine.
2. **Stop 1–3 reps short of failure** on every working set. Hard enough that the last rep is slow and ugly-ish, not so hard you're spotting yourself with your ego. Grinding to actual failure every set adds fatigue faster than it adds muscle.
3. **Rep ranges are targets, not laws.** 6–12 for the heavy compound work, 10–15 for isolation. A set of 13 when the target was 12 is a win, not an error.
4. **Warm up on the rower** — 5 minutes easy, then one or two light feeder sets of the first exercise before the working weight.
5. **Rest 2–3 minutes** between compound sets, 60–90 seconds on isolation. Shorter rests feel harder but grow less.

---

## Week 1 — Baseline week

No maxing out. You're returning after a break, so the first week's only job is to find working weights and let the joints re-acclimatise. For each exercise, pick a weight you can lift **8 times with 2–3 reps left in the tank**, do the prescribed sets, and log it. Hevy computes your estimated 1RM from any logged set — that estimate *is* the baseline, no failure test required.

Muscle memory is real: expect the numbers to climb fast for 6–8 weeks as you reclaim old ground. Do not chase that curve by cutting rest or maxing out — just follow rule 1 and let it happen.

## Progression and deloads

- **Double progression on everything.** Top of the rep range on all sets → add weight → rep count drops back → climb again. Hevy's exercise charts show the estimated-1RM line drifting up; that line rising is the plan working.
- **Every 6–8 weeks, or when three sessions in a row feel like a grind,** take a deload week: same exercises, same reps, **60% of the weights**. You'll come back stronger the following Monday.
- **Legs later, on purpose.** Upper-focus first is fine, but the optional Tue/Thu cardio keeps the legs and heart from atrophying in the meantime. Revisit in 8–12 weeks — a Mon push / Wed pull / Fri upper-plus-legs adjustment is the natural next step.

## Tape measure protocol

Every **two weeks, Monday morning** — before breakfast, before training, same conditions every time. Log the numbers in Hevy's measurements tab (or a `measurements.md` in this repo).

| Site | How |
|---|---|
| Chest | Nipple line, tape level, relaxed, mid-breath |
| Upper arm | Flexed, thickest point, same arm every time |
| Shoulders | Widest point around both deltoids, arms hanging |
| Waist | Navel level, relaxed — the sanity check that the scale weight going up is muscle-plus-normal, not just fat |

Fortnightly is deliberate: arm measurements move by millimetres a month, and weekly measuring is just noise that saps morale. Expect the first visible movement around week 6–8.

## Recovery — the third pillar

Protein is handled by the meal plans (56g before lunch on lift days already). The remaining lever is **sleep: 7–9 hours**. Muscle is built between sessions, not during them — a hard Monday session with five hours' sleep behind it is a worse stimulus than a decent session fully recovered. Creatine every day is already in the breakfast plan; that's the only supplement with evidence worth caring about.
